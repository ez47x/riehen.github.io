The two sides

Same postcode,
different worlds

Both carry the 4125 postcode, but north and south Riehen have entirely different characters. One feels like a village that forgot to become a suburb. The other didn't.

North Riehen

Vineyards, forest
& border land

Rolling vineyards that cross into Germany, the Herrenwald forest, the quirky Eiserne Hand border protrusion, and hiking trails that straddle two countries. Rural, quiet, and genuinely its own place — not just a suburb of Basel.

Eiserne Hand St. Chrischona Wenkenhof Herrenwald Hintere Gärten Fondation Beyeler
South Riehen

River walk,
tram stops & suburbs

The Wiese River promenade, leafy residential streets, and quick tram and train access into Basel. Pleasant and livable — but more of a gateway to the city than a destination in itself. It quietly dissolves into Basel.

Forest · Border

Eiserne Hand

Riehen's famous "Iron Hand" — a 2 km protrusion of Swiss territory jutting into Germany. Marked by centuries-old boundary stones hidden along forest paths. One of the most unusual geographic quirks in the region.

🎨

Museum · World-class

Fondation Beyeler

One of the finest private art collections in Europe. Set in a stunning Renzo Piano building surrounded by a reflecting pool and parkland. Monet, Picasso, Warhol, Giacometti — all in Riehen. A genuine cultural jewel.

⛪

Landmark · Pilgrimage

St. Chrischona

The dominant landmark of north Riehen — a church perched high above the city visible for miles. A pilgrimage destination since medieval times, with sweeping views over Riehen, Basel, and into Germany. The defining silhouette of the north.

🏛

Historic Estate

Wenkenhof

An elegant 18th-century estate with formal gardens and a distinguished history. Set quietly among the northern landscape, Wenkenhof is Riehen's most refined landmark — understated, aristocratic, and distinctly Swiss.

🌾

Hiking · Cross-border

Markgräfler Wiiwegli

A hiking trail through cross-border vineyards that weaves between Switzerland and Germany through the Markgräflerland wine country. Walk through vines in two countries in a single afternoon — golden in autumn.

🌳

Garden · Quiet

Hintere Gärten

Tucked away in north Riehen, the Hintere Gärten is a quiet residential garden area with beautiful views and a distinctly village feel. The kind of spot locals know about and tourists walk straight past.

🚶

Promenade · River

Wiesendamm Promenade

A popular cycling and running path along the Wiese River connecting south Riehen to Basel. Pleasant greenery, families on weekends, and a gentle pace. Good for a morning run, not much else.

🏘

Neighbourhood · Historic

Dorf Riehen

The historic village centre of Riehen, sitting roughly between north and south. The Dorfstrasse, the Spielzeugmuseum, the old church — a reminder that Riehen was a proper village long before it became a municipality.

Getting around

Transport &
connectivity

North and south Riehen are connected to Basel differently — and that difference shapes how each side feels to live in.

North Riehen

Riehen Bahnhof

The main station of Riehen sits in the north. S-Bahn connections into Basel and onward into Germany via DB. The station gives the north a quiet but genuine rail presence — useful, unhurried, and very Swiss.

S6 32 35

Line 32 serves the villa quarter and forest area — scenic but low frequency (every 20–50 min)

South Riehen

Niederholz Bahnhof

South Riehen's station sits close to the Basel border. Combined with the tram 6 running every 12 minutes into the city, it makes the south the more commuter-friendly half — fast and functional, but that accessibility is part of why it feels less like its own place.

6 34 S6

Tram 34 runs through the dense core of Riehen every 12 min — the backbone of south connectivity

The conclusion

So which side
actually wins?

Forget shared parks and border zones. Looking only at 4125 — its streets, its feel, its soul.

Winner: North

North 4125 has identity.
South 4125 has a tram stop.

Why North wins

The vineyards, the Eiserne Hand, St. Chrischona on the horizon, the Fondation Beyeler, and paths that cross into Germany — north Riehen is genuinely rare for a municipality this close to a major city. It has things worth seeking out. It has a soul.

Why South falls short

South 4125 is pleasant, functional, and well-connected. But strip away Lange Erlen and the Wiese (which belong to Basel anyway), and what remains is quiet residential streets and a train station. It's a great place to live. It's not a great place to explore. And that difference matters.
